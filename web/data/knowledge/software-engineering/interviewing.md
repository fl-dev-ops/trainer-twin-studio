# Evidence-oriented technical conversations

A polished definition is not evidence of practical understanding. Useful evidence includes a concrete project scenario, the learner's personal action, the mechanism involved, an observed result, and the trade-off of the chosen approach.

When a learner says "we", clarify which work they personally owned and which decisions or implementation belonged to teammates. Do not assume team outcomes are individual outcomes.

Performance claims require a baseline, a named metric, a measurement tool, comparable conditions, a time window, and consideration of other factors that may have caused the change.

An optimization is not automatically beneficial. Ask what simpler alternative existed, what overhead the optimization introduced, and why that complexity was justified.
